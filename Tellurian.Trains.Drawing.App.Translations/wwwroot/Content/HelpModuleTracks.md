# Tracks

The *Tracks* pane lists the tracks of the module that trains are planned on, as a timetable planner sees them. Every module has at least one: a through track or a track ending. A module drawn from a template gets its tracks in the list by itself, called 1, 2 and so on from the side you look from.

- **Add track** adds a track to the list. **Remove track** removes the selected one; its track elements stay in the drawing.
- Click a track's designation to see and change its properties.

## The properties of a track

- **Designation**: what the track is called, e.g. *1* or *2a*. Each track of the module has its own.
- **Length**: the length a train can use, in metres. It is worked out from the drawing: at a turnout the track ends where a train on the other track can still pass freely, not at the points. Untick **Length from the drawing** to type another length.
- **Platform length**: in metres; 0 when the track has no platform.
- **Direction**: the direction trains run on the track, from one end of the module to the other, or both.
- **Speed limit** and **Usage**: optional.
- **Main track**, **Scheduled** (trains are planned to use the track) and **Lockable**.
- **Stub track**: a track that can be entered from one end only. It is worked out from the drawing: a track with an end that isn't joined to anything and isn't at an end of the module. Untick **Stub track from the drawing** to choose yourself.

## Track elements

Each track element of the drawing is part of one track of the list, or of none. Choose the track for each element; point at a row to see the element in the drawing. A turnout is shared by the tracks that meet in it: give it to one of them, usually the main track. The track on each side of the turnouts can be part of the main track too; it doesn't add to the length.

The distance at which trains pass freely comes from the module's standard, and is set in the catalogue.
